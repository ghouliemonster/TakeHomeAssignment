Design Notes
Why this mechanic: When my daughter was younger, her favorite toys always paired a visual change with a sound the moment she did something, that immediate cause-and-effect feedback seemed to genuinely help her attention and curiosity as a young child. I wanted Firefly Jar's added mechanic to work the same way: catching a firefly doesn't just fill the jar, it also activates a frog or a cricket at the edge of the scene with its own little animated reaction and sound. Because different-colored fireflies wake different animals, a child gets to notice the pattern purely through repeated play. No reading, no way to get it "wrong," just a small, satisfying visual-plus-audio payoff every time.
What I'd build next: A third animal tied to the green fireflies, to complete the set the color palette already sets up. I'd also want more time to tune the celebration moment, the firework-style burst reads as more intense than the calm, floaty tone everywhere else in the game, and I'd either soften it or swap in something gentler once I had a proper polish pass.  Adding stars in the night sky and/or the moon.  I’d also cleanup the background image too, I did that quickly in Clip Studio Paint.
What I cut: I originally planned for the audio from the frog and cricket to play by having the fireflies blink at different rates.
